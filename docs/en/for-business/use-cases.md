# COS use cases

COS is most useful when a process has **several people, important data, decisions and movement between systems**.

## Sales

A sales process can connect the first request with qualification, assignment, follow-up, negotiation and the final result.

The value is not another contact list. The value is making sure every live opportunity has an owner and a clear next step.

## Real estate

Property operations often mix the physical asset, price, availability, marketing and publication data.

COS can keep those concepts separate while connecting them into one working process. This is useful for agencies, developers, property managers and large catalogs.

## Business diagnostics

A diagnostic process can collect facts, run structured interviews, compare evidence with criteria, find weak areas and keep the reasons behind each conclusion.

This turns an audit from “an expert has a feeling” into a process that can be repeated and checked.

## Customer service

A customer request can become a managed case with an owner, priority, response target, communication history, escalation rules and a clear outcome.

## Construction and project work

COS can coordinate stages, contractors, materials, documents, approvals and actual completion.

The practical approach is to automate one process at a time rather than trying to digitize an entire construction business in one heroic weekend.

## Procurement, finance, HR and documents

These can become separate COS domains when they participate in real cross-company processes.

For example, a sale may create a procurement need, procurement may require financial approval, and completion may create a document and a task.

## Choosing the first use case

A strong first process:

- matters to the business;
- already creates measurable loss of time, money or opportunities;
- can be isolated and tested with real cases.
