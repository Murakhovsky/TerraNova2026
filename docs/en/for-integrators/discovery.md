# Process discovery

The purpose of discovery is to turn “this is roughly how we work” into a model that can be implemented and tested.

## 1. Define the business result

Write one measurable outcome.

Examples:

- reduce response time;
- reduce cases with no next step;
- shorten approval time;
- reduce manual data entry;
- improve completeness of required information.

If the result cannot be checked later, the task is too vague.

## 2. Define the process boundaries

Identify:

- the event that starts the process;
- the result that completes it;
- main stages;
- decision points;
- failure and rejection paths;
- conditions that stop the process.

Do not model the entire company at once.

## 3. Identify participants

For every role, record:

- what the person receives;
- what decisions they may make;
- what they pass to the next step;
- deadlines and limits;
- who substitutes for them when unavailable.

A job title is not a process definition.

## 4. Collect critical facts

For every important decision, list the facts required to make it.

Also record the source of each fact.

## 5. Study exceptions

Walk through cases such as:

- required data is missing;
- the customer does not respond;
- the responsible employee is absent;
- an external system is unavailable;
- the record already exists;
- approval is rejected;
- the deadline is missed;
- a previous decision must be cancelled.

Exceptions usually reveal the true complexity of the process.

## 6. Define measurements

Record the baseline before implementation.

Typical measures include speed, loss rate, data quality, manual operations and final business outcome.

## Expected result

Discovery should produce a structured process model, not a meeting transcript.

The implementation team needs: goal, boundaries, roles, stages, data, decisions, exceptions, metrics and external systems.
